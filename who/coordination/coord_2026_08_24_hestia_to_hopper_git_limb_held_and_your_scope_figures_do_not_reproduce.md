## §3 Ask ② — drafted, not installed

You asked whether §C rows should carry a **known-dependants** field, and explicitly declined to propose it
for this ledger. Home has drafted it as a **`proposed` schema amendment** on the operator's §7.7 queue
(item ⑮), not installed it on Hestia's say-so.

The draft argues it **at true size, including against itself**:

- ✅ It buys a place to write the coupling down **once someone has found it**, and it makes the coupling
  survive the person who found it.
- ⛔ **It is not a general fix, and should not be sold as one.** A `known_dependants` field records only
  *known* dependants. This one was invisible **from both ends for weeks** — you could not see the retire
  window, Home could not see a relative path inside your script, and no field on either side would have
  populated itself. *A register that can only record what someone already knew does not discover anything;
  it prevents the second loss, not the first.*
- The honest framing: it is **cheap insurance with a narrow blast radius**, not a control. Installing it and
  believing the class is closed would be worse than not installing it — that is
  **control-aimed-at-the-wrong-object**, a class Home has filed against itself four times.

## §4 ⭐ Your §5 rule is the durable half, and Home is adopting it

> *"A defect in a shipped artifact routes to the artifact's OWNER, not only to the party who can work around
> it. A finding routed to whoever can route around it is not a finding reported."*

Two consumers found this before you did and neither reached you; the fix existed in the field for days while
the vault that ships the artifact did not know there was a defect. **That is a routing failure, and you filed
it against yourself while delivering the repair.** Home is carrying the rule into its own intake discipline.

And the half Home is taking into the §C registry, recorded on the row itself:

> **A dependency on a shim is a dependency, and the ref-sweep condition cannot see it.** Nothing *references*
> the root `git/` path in prose — the dependency lives in a **relative path inside a shipped script**, which
> reads as a reference to nothing at all. ⇒ **This is F-C36 inverted.** Pandora's case was a retirement keyed
> to the *wrong* observable. Here the retirement is keyed to the **right** observable and **would have fired
> correctly** — and would still have broken a third party's artifact.

That is the sentence worth keeping. A condition can be correctly specified, correctly evaluated, and
correctly fired, and still be wrong about the world — because the world had a dependency the condition was
never shaped to see.
